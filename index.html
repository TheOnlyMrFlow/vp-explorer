Three-point perspective: vanishing point explorer

Rotate, lean and move a box, look up or down, tilt your head and change the focal length. The three vanishing points are not independent: they move together, and the readout at the bottom shows the constraint that links them.

How to read it

  • P is the center of vision: the point on the picture plane directly in front of the eye. It is not necessarily the center of your page.
  • Measured from P, the dot product of any two vanishing points equals −f², where f is the distance from the eye to the picture plane. If one vanishing point is close to P, the others must be far away.
  • The three vanishing points form an acute triangle whose orthocenter is P. The dashed lines from each vanishing point to P are the altitudes of that triangle.
  • With the view level, the vertical vanishing point is at infinity and you get two-point perspective. Tilting the view brings it in, and the horizon moves away from P.
  • Tilting your head (roll) rotates the whole picture around P. The horizon tilts and the vertical vanishing point leaves the vertical line through P, but it stays on the line through P that is perpendicular to the horizon (while the box stands upright), and every constraint above still holds.
  • The horizon is your eye level. It depends only on where the camera looks (look down / up and head tilt), never on the box. A box that stands flat on the ground has horizontal edges that run parallel to the ground, so VP₁ and VP₂ land on the horizon.
  • Use the lean sliders under Box orientation to tip the box over. It no longer stands on the ground, so its edges point in new directions and all three vanishing points move. VP₁ and VP₂ leave the horizon, while the horizon itself stays where it was. This is why the vanishing points of a tipped box in a drawing are not on the horizon line.
  • What never changes is the link between the three vanishing points themselves. The three edges of a box always meet at right angles, so the dot-product rule and the triangle with P as its orthocenter hold for a leaning box too. Only the tie to the horizon needs the box to be upright.
  • Resizing the box never moves the vanishing points. They depend only on the directions of its edges, not on its length, width or height.
  • Moving the box left, right, up, down, closer or farther never moves the vanishing points either. It changes which faces you see and how strongly they are foreshortened, but the lines still converge on the same three points.
  • A small f means a wide-angle view with strong distortion. A large f means a telephoto view with nearly parallel lines.
