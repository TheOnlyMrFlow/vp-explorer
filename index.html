Three-point perspective: vanishing point explorer

Rotate, lean and move a box, look up or down, tilt your head and change the focal length. The three vanishing points are not independent: they move together, and the readout at the bottom shows the constraint that links them.

How to read it

  • P is the center of vision: the point straight ahead of the eye. It is not necessarily the center of your page.
  • The three vanishing points are linked on the picture plane (the page), not in 3D space. At most one can sit closer to P than f, the distance from your eye to the picture plane. The closer it gets, the farther the other two must go. The dashed lines from each one to P are the altitudes of their triangle.
  • The horizon is your eye level. It depends only on where the camera looks, never on the box. With the view level, the vertical vanishing point is at infinity (two-point perspective).
  • If the box is level, with its top and bottom faces parallel to the ground, VP₁ and VP₂ sit on the horizon. Lean it and its edges no longer run parallel to the ground, so they leave the horizon. The three points stay linked because the edges still meet at right angles.
  • Resizing or moving the box never moves the vanishing points. Only the direction of its edges does.
