Three-point perspective: vanishing point explorer

Rotate a box, look up or down, tilt your head and change the focal length. The three vanishing points are not independent: they move together, and the readout at the bottom shows the constraint that links them.

  • VP₁ (box length)
  • VP₂ (box width)
  • VP₃ (vertical)

How to read it

  • P is the center of vision: the point on the picture plane directly in front of the eye. It is not necessarily the center of your page.
  • Measured from P, the dot product of any two vanishing points equals −f², where f is the distance from the eye to the picture plane. If one vanishing point is close to P, the others must be far away.
  • The three vanishing points form an acute triangle whose orthocenter is P. The dashed lines from each vanishing point to P are the altitudes of that triangle.
  • With the view level, the vertical vanishing point is at infinity and you get two-point perspective. Tilting the view brings it in, and the horizon moves away from P.
  • Tilting your head (roll) rotates the whole picture around P. The horizon tilts and the vertical vanishing point leaves the vertical line through P, but it stays on the line through P that is perpendicular to the horizon, and every constraint above still holds.
  • Resizing the box never moves the vanishing points. They depend only on the directions of its edges, not on its length, width or height.
  • A small f means a wide-angle view with strong distortion. A large f means a telephoto view with nearly parallel lines.
